--- the per-miss question is settled, and the answer is "not constant" -----

The earlier record found the cost per miss differing by 28 % between the
arms at n=2 and could not say whether that was real batching behaviour or a
contaminated number. Dividing cpu-miss by the same run's miss count gives an
index, not a cost -- the numerator is per decode token and the denominator
spans the whole run -- but the index is comparable across arms:

    COLD    0.519    range 0.515 to 0.523   six runs
    CROSS   0.629    range 0.619 to 0.635   six runs

THE RANGES DO NOT OVERLAP, across six runs each. The 21 % gap is real and
reproducible. Fewer misses are individually more expensive, which is what
smaller CPU fallback groups would produce, and it means no per-miss cost
from either arm extrapolates to the other.

--- hit rate at a longer generation ----------------------------------------

At N_NEW=128 rather than 64 the CROSS arm reaches 87.9 % rather than 89.2 %,
and COLD 68.9 % rather than 68.3 %. The earlier record listed the behaviour
of the cross-topic hit rate at longer generations as unmeasured and said it
could fall (more subject matter) or rise (more of the generic hot core). It
falls, by 1.3 points over a doubling. One data point on one pair.

The swap count rises from 4 to 8 in both arms, which is the policy ceiling
again: 152 token positions give 9 attempts at one per 16 tokens, and 8 were
taken. See issue #47.
