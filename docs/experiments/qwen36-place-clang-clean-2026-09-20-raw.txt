The control rows move on clang and are FLAT on gcc, in the same session at the

Two readings, and the first one is the reason these counters exist.

THE KERNEL WINDOW IS NOT ALL KERNEL. cA and gA issue the same 2704 calls over
the same 92.37 GB to the same card, and the window between the two events is
475 ms against 750 -- 1.58x, from changing the HOST compiler. A GPU does not
know what compiled its host. What it can know is when the launch arrives:
ev[1] is recorded right after the H2D memcpy and ev[2] after the launch, both
on stream 0, so any delay in the CPU ISSUING the launch is GPU idle time
sitting inside the measured "kernel" interval. About 275 ms of gcc's kernel
time is the host failing to feed the card -- a spinning 16-thread libgomp team
against the driver's own threads, which is the contention this repo's OpenMP
record hypothesised and never measured. Read every "kernel GB/s" here as an
upper bound on the time, a lower bound on the bandwidth.

THE ROUND-TRIP IS NOT THE PROBLEM. It is 4-9 % of wall. coli_cuda_matmul being
synchronous three times per call is real and it is not what these GEMVs are
spending their time on. The cost is inside the kernel window: partly host
contention, and the rest a kernel running at 194 GB/s against the card's 672 --
29 % of peak, on the toolchain that feeds it properly.

So the claim that opened the dense-counter work -- that placed GEMVs run at
10-20 % of the card -- survives with better numbers. The 137 GB/s that
motivated it was a gcc-contaminated floor; 194 is the figure on a sane build,
and it is still 3.5x short of the hardware.

One structural detail worth keeping: arm B issues 93 % MORE calls for only
21 % more bytes, because dnout and attnout are smaller matrices than dnproj,
and its round-trip share doubles from 4 % to 8 %. Placing many small matrices
costs more overhead per byte than placing few large ones.
