--- what stands in for counterbalancing, and what does not ----------------

The arms CANNOT be alternated. A heat table has to be built before it can be
used, so the order cold -> warm is forced by the mechanism. Three things were
offered in its place. The second does not survive; it is left here, struck
through in words, because a reader who wants to trust the range above needs
to see which argument was withdrawn and why.

  1. THE ROUTING COUNTERS ARE EXACTLY REPRODUCIBLE. Not close: equal.

       COLD  prompt25       uploads 6963  miss(CPU) 8916   runs 1, 2
       CROSS prompt25       hits 25114    miss(CPU) 3046   runs 6, 8
       SELF  prompt25       hits 28160    miss(CPU)    0   runs 3, 4, 9

     Run 7 (COLD prompt-caldo, hits 20102, miss 9338) is NOT listed with
     them: run 5 was its only possible repeat and was never captured, so it
     is n=1 and has nothing to be equal to. Runs 1 and 2 are compared on
     `uploads` and `miss(CPU)` rather than `hits`, because run 1's
     `dev 0: hits` line was not captured.

     This is an OBSERVATION at n=2 and n=3, not a property that follows
     from the mechanism. Greedy argmax decoding on a fixed prompt is the
     likely reason, but the cold arm's resident set is NOT fixed: it takes
     four LFRU swaps per run, and those are asynchronous -- the victim is
     marked non-resident immediately (c/qwen36_tier.c:1048) while the
     promotion is queued for the uploader thread, so the window in which the
     newcomer is still missing is scheduler-dependent and lookups inside it
     count as misses. That the counters came out equal anyway is a result,
     not a guarantee. What follows from it is only that the scatter in the
     timing columns is timing scatter.

  2. THE COLD-ARM SPREAD BOUNDS THE SHORT-RANGE NOISE. 32.8 vs 31.9 on
     identical configuration, back to back, is 0.9 ms/token. The cross-topic
     gap is 4.8. What this does NOT bound is the session-long drift dealt
     with above; an earlier version of this section claimed the cold arm
     bracketed the warm one and that the machine did not drift. Both were
     false: runs 8 and 9 come after run 7, so nothing brackets anything, and
     the three cold values fall monotonically. The claim is withdrawn and
     replaced by the range in the result section.

  3. THE SUB-TIMERS ARE TIGHTER THAN THE TOTALS. conv 0.4-0.5 and l2n+rec
     1.8-2.0 across the eight runs that have a dn-sub line, against 0.9 of
     scatter on step() in the cold pair alone. Whatever moves the totals
     between runs is not moving DeltaNet's CPU work.

--- three findings that are not the headline ------------------------------

WITHOUT A HEAT TABLE THE RESIDENT SET IS A LAYER PREFIX. THAT BOUNDS THE
COLD HIT RATE; IT DOES NOT DETERMINE IT.

  resident 6959 / 10240 = 67.96 %
  cold hit rate         = 68.34 %  on prompt25   (19244 / 28160)
                        = 68.28 %  on prompt-caldo (20102 / 29440)

  An earlier version of this file read that near-equality as proof that
  routing is unpredictable -- "what picking experts at random would give" --
  and then called it an identity that "could not have produced another
  number". Both are wrong. The code gives the correct, weaker statement.

  With no heat table qt_plan_fill sets fill_order[i]=i (c/qwen36_tier.c:961)
  and decodes it as l = gi/G.ne (:969), so the budget is consumed in strict
  global index order: a LAYER-MAJOR PREFIX. THAT REQUIRES ONE DEVICE, which
  this run has. Experts are homed by eid % G.ndev (:72), and with more than
  one device qt_plan_fill skips (:972) an expert whose home is full while
  filling the others, breaking only when every device is full (:966-967) --
  a per-device prefix, not a contiguous global one, and the arithmetic below
  would not hold. 6959 = 27*256 + 47, so layers
  0-26 are entirely resident, layer 27 holds 47 of 256, and layers 28-39
  hold NOTHING -- at the start. Four LFRU swaps mutate the set during the
  run (uploads 6963 against resident 6959 is exactly four promotions), so
  everything below describes the INITIAL fill and is approximate to that
  extent. On that fill 27 layers hit and 12 miss, and the hit rate is

        (27 + p) / 40,   p = share of layer-27 routes landing in expert 0-46

  BOUNDED TO [67.5 %, 70.0 %] whatever the routing does -- on the initial
  fill -- and equal to the residency fraction only when p = 47/256 = 18.4 %,
  which is a claim about the routing distribution rather than a structural
  fact.

  It does not hold. Inverting the two measured rates gives

        prompt25       p = 33.5 %      against 18.4 % uniform, +83 %
        prompt-caldo   p = 31.3 %      against 18.4 % uniform, +70 %

  and an earlier version of this file called that margin "slight". The
  prompt25 excess is about 107 extra hits out of 704 layer-27 lookups
  (8 x 88 tokens); the prompt-caldo one about 95 out of 736 (8 x 92). An
  earlier version of this file printed 91 there, which is the prompt-caldo
  excess share against the prompt25 denominator.

  THE FOUR LFRU SWAPS ARE NOT RULED OUT AS THE CAUSE, and an earlier version
  of this file claimed they were, by comparing 107 against 704 -- a ratio
  that says nothing about swap capacity. Computed properly: attempts land at
  tokens 16, 32, 48, 64 and 80, four are taken, and a promoted expert is
  resident for the rest of the run, so four promotions cover between 128 and
  192 subsequent lookups depending on which attempts took, and 144 to 208
  on the 92-token prompt. The smallest of those exceeds both excesses.
  Since LFRU promotes the hottest non-resident experts, the swaps are a
  live candidate, and the promotions are not confined to layer
  27 -- a promoted expert in layers 28-39 would add hits that this inversion
  charges to p.

  So p is an upper bound on layer-27 index skew, not a measurement of it.
  Something makes the cold hit rate exceed the uniform-routing floor by
  0.3-0.4 points on both prompts; whether it is routing skew, the four
  swaps, or both, nothing here separates them.

  What survives is that the near-agreement of 68.34 % and 68.28 % across two
  unrelated prompts is mostly structural -- the prefix pins 27 of the 40
  layers -- and only partly a coincidence of p.

  What can be said about natural order is narrower than "worth nothing",
  which an earlier version of this file claimed after withdrawing the only
  argument that could have supported it. NO ARBITRARY-ORDER BASELINE WAS
  EVER MEASURED, so nothing here compares natural order to a shuffle. What
  is measured is that heat order reaches 89.2 % on a prompt it has never
  seen at the SAME 6959 slots, against 68.3 %: natural order leaves about
  21 points of hit rate on the table. That is a comparison at fixed
  residency, and it is the one that carries the claim.

"LFRU swaps 4" IS BOUNDED BY THE POLICY AT 5, NOT BY THE WORKLOAD.

  qt_lfru_tick_locked (c/qwen36_tier.c:1030) is called once per token
  (qt_issue, layer 0, c/qwen36_tier.c:1071). Lines :1032-1036 verbatim, with
  nothing elided -- an earlier version of this file quoted only the last two
  and dropped the tick counter and the 1024-tick decay without an ellipsis:

    G.tick++;
    if(!(G.tick%1024))
        for(size_t i=0;i<n;i++) G.slot[i].heat=tier_decay_value(G.slot[i].heat);
    if(G.tick%16) return;
    for(int di=0;di<G.ndev;di++){

  So: one attempt every 16 tokens, and the loop body below it admits at most
  one swap per device. Those two readings are this record's; the source's own
  comment is at :1027-1029 and says the same in its own words.

  88 tokens of work on prompt25 (25 prefill + 63 decode), 92 on prompt-caldo,
  gives 5 attempts on one device. Every cold run captured reports 4 swaps, so
  THE CEILING IS 5 AND ONE ATTEMPT DECLINED. The record cannot say which of
  the three exits took it: no eligible cold/hot pair on the device, the
  admission test tier_should_promote requiring hot > cold + cold/4 + 4
  (c/tier.h:9-12, a comparison of heat values and therefore of the
  workload), or the queue-full revert at :1050. So 4 is jointly determined
  by policy and workload; only the 5 is pure policy.

  The ceiling of 5 carries a precondition. G.swaps has a second increment
  site, stream_promote_locked at :878, which is not gated by the 16-tick
  cadence. It cannot fire here because it sits behind G_fp8_stream
  (:888-891), set only by qt_init_fp8 (:470-471) and never for an int4
  gs64 container. On an fp8 container the bound does not hold.

  With 3281 experts non-resident and 8916 lookups missing on prompt25, the
  adaptive tier moves four of them. At one expert per 16 tokens
  it would need on the order of 52,000 tokens to repair a bad initial set, so
  in a short session the online policy cannot compensate for the cold start.
  This is a ceiling, not a bug report: whether it SHOULD move faster is a
  question about upload bandwidth and ping-pong that this record does not
  answer.

  columns have different denominators and their ratio means nothing.

  Nor are there three miss levels. Run 7 is on the longer prompt, and per
  token it misses at the SAME rate as the cold prompt25 runs:

        9338 / 92 tokens = 101.50 misses per token   run 7
        8916 / 88 tokens = 101.32 misses per token   runs 1, 2
        3046 / 88 tokens =  34.61 misses per token   runs 6, 8

  a difference of 0.18 %. There are two non-zero levels, not three, and
  run 7 adds only a prompt mix. An earlier version of this file also called
  the cost "monotone in the miss count", which its own rows refute: run 2
  has 8916 misses at 7.96 ms/token and run 7 has 9338 at 7.91 -- more misses,
  less cost. Monotonicity survives only against the run 1-2 mean, by
  0.01 ms/token, against 0.13 of scatter inside that same pair.

  What the table supports is only this: 2.9x the misses per token costs 2.3x
  the time, so the relationship is sublinear. Separating prefill from decode
  misses would need a counter that does not exist yet.

--- what this run did NOT measure -----------------------------------------

  - Anything about current main, and the deltas are NOT expected to carry
    over unchanged. Placing attnproj does not only make attention faster: it
    charges 0.17 GB of trunk to the expert budget, 6959 residents down to
    6859 (qwen36-attnproj-place-2026-09-22-raw.txt:196-197), moving the
    residency fraction from 67.96 % to 66.98 %. Residency is precisely the
    variable this record says its result must depend on, in the bullet
    beginning "Any other model, quantisation, GPU, or residency fraction".
    A rebuild therefore changes the measured quantity, not just the baseline
    it is measured against. How much is unknown.
  - Longer generations. N_NEW=64 with a 25-token prompt is 88 tokens of
    routing (25 prefill + 63 decode: the first generated token comes out
    of prefill, which is why the engine's block reads "decode: 63
    tokens"). A longer answer touches more experts and the cross-topic
    hit rate at 512 or 2048 tokens is unknown; it could fall (more subject
    matter) or rise (more of the generic hot core).
  - An accumulated heat table. The warm arms here are built from very little
    history: runs 3 and 6 load a table written by a single ~88- or ~92-token
    run, while run 4's table also carries run 3 and run 9's carries runs 7
    and 8 (two subjects). What the file does on load is easy to get wrong,
    so precisely: c/qwen36_tier.c:617 halves it into the runtime slots
    (G.slot[i].heat = G.heat0[i] >> 1), but G.heat0 itself keeps the file's
    UNDECAYED values, and G.heat0 is what orders the warmstart (:962,
    cmp_heat_desc) and what auto_place receives (:625). The resident set --
    the only thing this record measures -- is therefore chosen from the
    undecayed table. The halving does not touch this session's INITIAL
    resident set -- but it governs every change to it, because
    qt_lfru_tick_locked compares the halved G.slot[].heat (:1042-1046) and
    tier_should_promote is hot > cold + cold/4 + 4 (c/tier.h:9-12), whose
    fixed +4 is NOT scale-invariant: halving both sides makes admission
    strictly harder in file units. Four swaps happened in every captured
    run. And it is not harmless afterwards either, because what :1241
    writes back at exit
    is G.slot[].heat, the halved copy plus this session's increments, and
    that file becomes the next session's undecayed G.heat0. So the halving
    is exactly what makes an accumulated table a geometrically decaying SUM
    of per-session routing counts rather than a running total; nothing
    normalises by the number of sessions, so it is not an average. The slots
    also halve again every 1024 ticks in-run (tier_decay_value, c/tier.h:14,
    applied at :1034). A table grown across many unrelated prompts is the
    obvious next experiment and was not run.
  - Run 9 is worth noting for that reason: its heat table contained BOTH
    subjects and still reached 100.0 % on prompt25. One prior exposure was
    enough, mixed with another topic's history. A single data point.
  - Any other model, quantisation, GPU, or residency fraction. 6959/10240 is
    68 % of the expert set in VRAM; the value of a heat table must depend on
    that fraction and only one value of it was tested.
  - Anything about the OTHER hit-rate counter, which moves the OPPOSITE
    way. Every run also prints a host `Expert cache hit rate`: 78.4 % in
    the cold runs and 79.1 % in run 7, against 75.3 % in CROSS and 73.3 %
    in SELF. A warm table makes that number look WORSE. It counts something
    else -- host RAM cache lookups, whose denominator falls when the CPU
    fallback path stops running -- and this record does not analyse it.
    The run table's `hit rate` column is the [qtier] VRAM figure throughout.
    Flagged because issue #44 section 2 is open about exactly this
    two-counter confusion and qwen36-autoplace-2026-09-22-raw.txt:266 warns
    that the host counter "is not evidence about what the GPU did".
  - Whether the COLD arm is the state it is named after. It is not static:
    four LFRU swaps mutate the resident set inside each of the three cold
    runs whose [qtier] block was captured (run 5's was not), so the
    68.3 % is an average over a set that changed four times, and the fifth
    declined attempt is unexplained.
  - Anything that would let decode be separated from prefill. step() and
    cpu-miss are per decode token; hits, miss(CPU), uploads and the swap
    count span the whole run. No counter splits them.
  - Cold NVMe. Every run loaded from a filesystem cache warmed by the
    previous ones. Run 1 is the closest to cold and is also the slowest of
    the cold runs (22.83 tok/s vs 24.07); it is included in the mean, which
    makes the reported gain slightly larger than it would be without it.
    Excluding run 1 gives COLD 31.9 and a gain of -4.35 ms/token. That
    figure is NOT stacked with the -0.85 of position charges that set the
    range's pessimistic end: run 1's slowness and a session warm-up are
    plausibly the same thing, so charging both would count it twice. The
    range's low end is derived under the position reading alone.

--- no manifest -----------------------------------------------------------

No docs/experiments/*.json manifest is written for this record, and the
reason is specific rather than a general exemption. c/experiment_manifest.py
validates exactly TWO arms -- _run(record,"baseline") and
_run(record,"trial") -- whose configs must differ in exactly one key named by
changed_variables, and it requires at least three tok/s samples per arm
(`len(speeds) < 3`). This measurement has THREE arms, and two of them (COLD
runs 1 and 2, CROSS runs 6 and 8) have two samples each. It cannot be
expressed in that schema without dropping an arm or inventing samples.

A blocker that reflects on this measurement rather than on the schema, and
which matters more: validate() requires `commit` to be a full 40-character
hex SHA (:58-66) and a hardware block (:67-69). No commit, build date or
digest exists for this binary, and the host line is carried over from another
record rather than observed. A truthful manifest could not be written even
for a two-arm subset.

The validator does NOT check alternation or run order; an earlier version of
this paragraph said it did, which was wrong. Nor is this the same case as
issue #44, which is about a metric in ms/token and a changed_variables that
names a binary rather than a config key; here the metric is available as
tok/s and the variable is HEAT_FILE, an environment key. The three-arm shape
and the two-sample arms are what block it.

Most qwen36 records in this directory are raw-only -- eleven of about
fourteen, everything since 19 September except qwen36-i8-rows. None of the
raw FILES states a reason; issue #44 section 1 records one for two of them
(qwen36-autoplace and qwen36-attnproj-place). No shared grounds are claimed
here on the other nine's behalf.
