Qwen3.6-35B-A3B: dnout+attnout placement, re-measured on a clang build.
19 September 2026.

The placement A/B in docs/qwen36-cuda-tier.md read -7.77 ms/token on a
gcc/MinGW-libgomp binary. The prediction on record was that the gain would
SHRINK on clang, because placement pays by removing CPU reads and libgomp
makes those reads cost about twice what libomp charges. This tests it.

Host: Ryzen 9 7950X, 64 GB DDR5-5200, RTX 4070 Ti SUPER 16 GB, Windows 11.
Model: Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Colibri int4 gs64. COLI_GPUS=0, 25-token prompt,
128 generated. Both binaries from the same commit via `make -B`.

  A  COLI_PLACE="experts=0,lmhead=0,dnproj=0"
  B  COLI_PLACE="experts=0,lmhead=0,dnproj=0,dnout=0,attnout=0"

2x2 {gcc, clang} x {A, B}, arms alternated cA cB gA gB within each repetition,
heat warmed then frozen and restored before every run. Unlike 19 September's
run this session settles at a 98.1-98.2 % VRAM hit rate rather than 100 %, so
arm B pays the expert-displacement cost that a fully resident session hides.

--- the gcc arm is not measurable by its mean -------------------------------

Four of the eight gcc repetitions are disturbed (1, 5, 7, 8), and in two of
them the B arm is SLOWER than A. Clang, which runs inside the same repetition
minutes apart, stays between 33.10 and 34.30 for all eight. So this is not the
machine slowing down under both: the gcc binary itself is unstable here, which
is consistent with the wider relative spread the OpenMP-runtime record already
reported for its gcc arm.

More repetitions do not fix a recurrent disturbance. What does is the PAIRED
delta: gA and gB run back to back inside a repetition, so their difference
absorbs whatever both are suffering.

    gcc   -11.70 -5.30 -5.10 -6.60 +1.50 -5.80 +10.20 -4.50  median -5.20

--- the prediction was wrong ------------------------------------------------

The absolute gain does NOT shrink on clang: -4.90 against gcc's -5.20. What
changes is its weight, and in the opposite direction to the prediction --
-14.7 % of a 33.4 ms token against roughly -9.5 % of a 55 ms one. Placement
matters MORE on a sane build, not less.

The row that says why:

  norm+out -> dnout    clang  5.50 -> 2.44   (-3.06)
                       gcc    9.31 -> 6.13   (-3.19)

Very different baselines, near-identical saving. What placement removes is one
large streaming GEMV, and that is DRAM-bandwidth bound -- DRAM does not know
which compiler built the host. libgomp's price is per-parallel-region overhead
(54 us a region, measured in the OpenMP record), which lands on the many small
operations -- router, shared expert, the gated norm -- not on the single large
read being displaced. The original mechanism, "libgomp makes those reads cost
twice as much so removing them saves twice as much", does not survive its own
row.

--- control rows ------------------------------------------------------------

shared +0.22, router +0.22, cpu-miss +0.31, where 19 September had them flat
(-0.09, +0.01, -0.09). cpu-miss has a candidate explanation: this session runs
at 98 % rather than 100 %, so arm B's 0.31 GB of displaced experts costs real
misses that a fully resident session hides. shared and router do not have one.
They are small against -4.90 and do not overturn the result, but they are a
difference from the earlier measurement and they are recorded, not explained.
