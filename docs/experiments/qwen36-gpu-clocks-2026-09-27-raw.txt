Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on the CUDA tier: where the dense GEMV time goes, and the
GPU power states behind it.
27 September 2026, one session on the operator's machine.

The session started as an A/B of one CPU-side change and ended somewhere
else. In order:

  1. COLI_DENSE_IDOT=1 (integer dots for the CPU dense GEMVs): no effect.
  2. One profiled run: the placed dense GEMVs cost ~133 us a call, the
     kernel ~255 GB/s.
  3. A benchmark of the same kernels with no engine around them: 480-620
     GB/s, about half the time per call.
  4. Four hypotheses for the gap, each tested and each rejected: an old
     kernel in the DLL, the spinning OpenMP team, the card idling between
     calls, a fresh output buffer per token.
  5. nvidia-smi and Nsight Systems: during decode the card drops to P3/P5
     after ~2 s, and the dense kernels themselves run at half speed.
  6. Locking both clocks with nvidia-smi: -2.07 ms/token, 3 of 3 pairs.
     Locking the memory clock alone: +1.80, 3 of 3. The driver's "Prefer
     maximum performance" for the program: no effect.

Everything below is from output the operator pasted in the session. The
logs stay on the operator's machine; none is committed and no
evidence.sha256 exists for them.

COLI_CUDA_PROFILE=1, one run. From the [qtier] and [timers] lines:

  dense_stats: 11038 calls, 229.92 GB (weights+scales) | h2d 175 ms,
               kernel 903 ms, d2h 217 ms, wall 1468 ms
               kernel 255 GB/s | wall 157 GB/s | round-trip 12 % of wall
  group_stats: 4470 calls, 33029 experts | h2d 58 ms, kernel 708 ms,
               d2h 37 ms

11038 = 127 x 81 + 25 x 30 + 1: 81 placed dense GEMVs per decode token
(30 dnproj, 30 dnout, 10 attnproj, 10 attnout, lm_head), plus the 25
prompt positions' dnproj and one prefill lm_head. That decomposition fits
the count; it is not printed by the engine. Per call: 133 us of wall, 82
of kernel.

--- 3. the kernel alone -----------------------------------------------------

tests/bench_dense_gemv_cuda.cu runs the same kernel (quant_matmul_launch)
on the shapes and matrix counts of one token, distinct matrices so L2 (48
MB) cannot serve them, for each COLI_CUDA_I8_ROWS width, three rounds.
Every width matched R=0 byte for byte and R=0 matched a double CPU
reference (rel rms 1.0e-07 to 1.1e-07) in all three builds. "batch" is
back-to-back launches, "single" one launch per synchronize, "call" the
engine's coli_cuda_matmul with both pageable copies. Per token, 81 calls,
median of three rounds, ms (first run, 46402074):

  R | batch | single kernel | single host | call
  0 |  7.69 |          8.38 |        9.11 | 9.74
  2 |  3.15 |          3.86 |        4.63 | 5.20
  4 |  3.21 |          3.91 |        4.63 | 5.27
  8 |  3.26 |          3.95 |        4.68 | 5.28

At R=2 the kernel reads 480-620 GB/s (lm_head 603-621 in batch). The two
later builds reran this table: R=2 call 5.43 and 5.46.

So a call that costs ~64 us alone (5.20 / 81) costs ~133 in the engine,
and the engine's 255 GB/s is close to the benchmark's R=0 (219-243 GB/s
batch). That raised the question of the DLL's default (section on the
DLL above: it very likely runs R=2).

--- 4. hypotheses tested and rejected ---------------------------------------

4a. The spinning OpenMP team (the "(c)" left open in
    qwen36-attnproj-place-2026-09-22-raw.txt:189, "KMP_BLOCKTIME=0
    discriminates (c)"). Profiled runs, alternated A B A B, A = default
    (libomp spins 200 ms), B = KMP_BLOCKTIME=0:

      run  step()  dense kernel  dense wall  kernel GB/s
      A1   33.1    903 ms        1473 ms     255
      B1   49.8    1072          1698        214
      A2   32.5    887           1449        259
      B2   50.4    1131          1764        203

    Letting the team sleep makes the token ~17 ms slower and the dense
    kernel slower too. The team is not what slows the dense path.

4b. The card idling between calls. The benchmark (bae8b0c8) spins the host
    0, 50, 200 or 500 us before each call, the gap not counted. Per token
    at R=2, call column: 5.48, 5.57, 5.79, 6.86 ms. A third build
    (30c63e86) read 6.15, 6.03, 6.12, 7.45 in a noisier session: in both
    builds single rounds of dnproj, dnout, attnproj and attnout ran up to
    twice as slow as their neighbours. Idle gaps cost something, far
    from the ~5 ms missing.

4c. A fresh output buffer. step() mallocs the vocab-sized logits (~1 MB)
    per token and the caller frees them. The benchmark (30c63e86) timed
    lm_head's call into a reused buffer, into a buffer malloc'd per call,
    and into the reused one plus a memcpy: 998-1002, 1000-1009 and
    1021-1024 us over three rounds. No effect.

4d. Memory clock alone: see section 6.

--- what this means ----------------------------------------------------------

On this machine the placed dense GEMVs run at about half speed for most of
a decode: about two seconds in, at 15-40 % utilization, the card drops to
P3, and the kernels take twice their P2 time. The expert kernels probably
slow down too; that was not read. The benchmark keeps the card busy and
stays in P2, which is why it and the engine disagreed by two. Earlier
records of this path in docs/experiments logged no clocks, so how much of
their decode ran in P3 is unknown.

That is also a candidate for the factor 2.1 between the kernel readings
of 20 and 21 September (docs/qwen36-cuda-tier.md, the note under the
dense round-trip table): a run spends a longer or shorter part of its
decode in P3 depending on its length and its phases. A candidate, not a
finding: no record of those days logged clocks.

Precedents in the repository for the same mechanism on other backends:
docs/vulkan.md:104 ("decode dispatches are microsecond bursts that never
ramp DPM on their own; the memory clock can sit parked through an entire
run", with power_dpm_force_performance_level=high as the pin), and
COLI_METAL_SPIN in c/backend_metal.mm:1020, a keep-alive that keeps
trivial GPU work in flight. Outside it, llama.cpp issue #17880 reports a
GeForce on Windows 11 stuck in P3 during inference, 25 against 37
tokens/s, with the Control Panel settings not helping.

--- what this run did NOT measure --------------------------------------------

  - Longer generations. At 128 tokens about half the decode was already
    at full clocks in the N arms; a long answer would spend almost all of
    it in P3, so the gain of 6b is probably larger in use. Not measured.
  - The heat table. Every run here is without one.
  - The generated text in sections 4 to 6 (no guard script compared it).
  - The expert kernels' own slowdown in P3: the graph path records no
    timing without COLI_CUDA_PROFILE, and Nsight's rows for it were not
    read.
  - The driver version, and whether "CUDA - Force P2 State" (NVIDIA
    Profile Inspector) or the global Control Panel setting behave
    differently from the per-program one.
  - A way to keep the clocks up without administrator rights.
