--- 4. hypotheses for the gap ------------------------------------------------

4a. The spinning OpenMP team. qwen36-attnproj-place-2026-09-22-raw.txt
    left open a "(c) launch latency and OpenMP" for another delta and
    proposed "Rerunning arm A alone with KMP_BLOCKTIME=0" (its line 189);
    this addresses the OpenMP half of that in a different setup.
    Profiled runs, alternated A B A B, A = default (libomp spins 200 ms),
    B = KMP_BLOCKTIME=0:

      run  step()  dense kernel  dense wall  kernel GB/s
      A1   33.1    903 ms        1473 ms     255
      B1   49.8    1072          1698        214
      A2   32.5    887           1449        259
      B2   50.4    1131          1764        203

    Stopping the spin does not speed the dense path; it slows it, and the
    token by ~17 ms. That does not support the spinning team as the
    cause, but it cannot reject it on its own: the B runs decode at ~50
    ms/token, so they spend longer at low utilization and, by section 5,
    longer in P3, which slows the kernel by itself. Two runs per arm,
    unpaired. The stronger argument against host issue delay is section
    5's Nsight table, measured on the GPU timeline.

4b. Short idle gaps between calls. The benchmark (bae8b0c8) spins the host
    0, 50, 200 or 500 us before each call, the gap not counted. Per token
    at R=2, call column: 5.48, 5.57, 5.79, 6.86 ms. A third build
    (30c63e86) read 6.15, 6.03, 6.12, 7.45 in a noisier session: in both
    builds single rounds of dnproj, dnout, attnproj and attnout ran up to
    twice as slow as their neighbours, possibly short P3 episodes (clocks
    were not logged during those runs). Gaps of at most 500 us inside
    stretches of under a second, between saturating sections, cost little.
    That is not the regime of section 5, where the card is under-used for
    seconds on end; no benchmark arm reproduced that.

4c. A fresh output buffer. step() mallocs the vocab-sized logits (~1 MB)
    per token and the caller frees them. The benchmark (30c63e86) timed
    lm_head's call into a reused buffer, into a buffer malloc'd per call,
    and into the reused one plus a memcpy: 998-1002, 1000-1009 and
    1021-1024 us over three rounds. No effect.

4d. Memory clock alone: see section 6.

their launches, a guess): ~100-125 us against ~244 today (7.32 ms/token
/ 30), a gain of roughly 3.5-4.3 ms/token (an estimate, inside the
plan's 2.7-4.5).

--- what this means ----------------------------------------------------------

On this machine, in every decode that was logged, the card holds P2 for a
burst of 1.5-2 s and then drops to P3, at 15-40 % utilization; when that
burst starts relative to the first decoded token is not marked. In P3 the
placed dense kernels take about twice their P2 time. Locking both clocks
brings lm_head back to its benchmark time. That accounts for much of the
kernel half of the benchmark-engine gap of section 3, how much is not
measured (no locked run was profiled); the copy and host half (~35 us a
call, measured profiled) is not addressed by it. The double lock recovered
2.07 ms/token, if that number holds, against ~5.6 missing (81 x 133 us
against 5.20 ms). The expert kernels probably slow down too; that was not
read. Earlier records of this path in docs/experiments logged no clocks,
so how much of their decode ran in P3 is unknown.

The keep-alive of section 7 held P2 without administrator rights in its
first run, the only one with a clock log. In its final version, step()
29.07 -> 25.62 ms/token, 95 % [-3.69, -3.21], six pairs of six, no
end-to-end cost visible in the log end times (to within 2-3 s). With
the heat table in both arms (section 8): -2.37, [-3.76, -0.98], 6/6;
repeated that evening (section 9a): -0.57, [-4.46, +3.33], 5/6, one
outlier run widening it; and the next morning (section 10): -1.77,
[-2.61, -0.93], 6/6.

It is a weak candidate for the factor 2.1 between the kernel readings of
20 and 21 September (docs/qwen36-cuda-tier.md, the note under the dense
round-trip table). Both readings are of the same 5224 calls over 64
tokens, a decode of ~2 s, which by this record would be mostly P2; and
2.1 is more than the 2 that P3 gives here. It would need one of those
runs to start already in P3, for instance right after another run. No
record of those days logged clocks.

Precedents in the repository for the same mechanism on other backends:
docs/vulkan.md:104-106 ("decode dispatches are microsecond bursts that
never ramp DPM on their own; the memory clock can sit parked through an
entire run", with power_dpm_force_performance_level=high as the pin), and
COLI_METAL_SPIN in c/backend_metal.mm:1020-1022, a keep-alive that keeps
trivial GPU work in flight, described there as an experiment to quantify
clock ramp-down, not as a measured fix. Outside it, llama.cpp issue #17880
(github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/17880, read through a web summary
in this session, not checked line by line) reports a GeForce on Windows
11 stuck in P3 during inference, 25 against 37 tokens/s, with the
Control Panel settings not helping.

--- what this run did NOT measure --------------------------------------------

  - Longer generations. At 128 tokens about half the decode was at full
    clocks in the N arms; a long answer would spend a larger share in P3.
    That any gain of 6b grows with length is an extrapolation.
  - step() under the double lock with enough pairs, in a balanced order.
  - A graphics-clock-only lock.
  - Throttle reasons (clocks_event_reasons), power and temperature: idle
    DVFS against power or thermal capping, in particular for 6a's graphics
    clock at ~1.1 GHz under a memory lock.
  - Clocks during the Nsight run, and kernel durations split by phase.
  - The per-call breakdown of section 2 without the profiler, and whether
    P3 also slows the small h2d/d2h copies.
  - The power and thermal cost of running locked.
  - Replication after a reboot, and of sections 1-6 in another
    session (section 10 repeats only the keep-alive, the next morning).
  - The heat table in sections 1-7: every run there is without one.
  - The generated text in sections 2 to 6 (no guard script compared it).
  - The expert kernels' own slowdown in P3: the graph path records no
    timing without COLI_CUDA_PROFILE, and Nsight's rows for it were not
    read.
  - The driver version, and whether "CUDA - Force P2 State" (NVIDIA
    Profile Inspector) or the global Control Panel setting behave
    differently from the per-program one.
  - Clocks in sections 8 to 11. How each deltanet GPU call splits
    between round trip and kernel is measured in section 11, once, with
    the kernel from a profiled run and the wall from an unprofiled one.
  - Clocks in the keep-alive's second and third runs: that the final
    version, with its 50 ms pauses on frees, holds P2 is inferred from
    its step() times, not logged.
  - The keep-alive on another card, another driver, or Linux.
  - The keep-alive's power draw, its effect with a heat table beyond
    sections 8, 9a and 10 (three runs of six pairs), and on long
    generations.
