Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on the CUDA tier: where the dense GEMV time goes, and the
GPU power states behind it.
27 September 2026, one session on the operator's machine.

The session started as an A/B of one CPU-side change and ended somewhere
else. In order:

  1. COLI_DENSE_IDOT=1 (integer dots for the CPU dense GEMVs): no
     measurable effect on ms/token.
  2. One profiled run: the placed dense GEMVs cost ~133 us a call, the
     kernel ~255 GiB/s as the engine prints it.
  3. A benchmark of the same kernels with no engine around them: 482-621
     GB/s back to back at R=2, about half the time per call.
  4. Hypotheses for the gap: the DLL running the old kernel (no: Nsight's
     grids show R=2), the spinning OpenMP team (not supported, with a
     confound), sub-millisecond idle gaps between calls (small), a fresh
     output buffer per token (no).
  5. nvidia-smi and Nsight Systems: during each logged decode the card
     holds P2 for a burst of 1.5-2 s, then drops to P3 (memory 5001 MHz,
     graphics ~1700-2050 MHz); the dense kernels' median duration is
     twice their minimum.
  6. Locking both clocks with nvidia-smi: lm_head and deltanet lower in
     every locked run than in every unlocked one (lm_head 1.05-1.11 ms
     against 1.28-1.69); step() -2.07 ms/token, 3 of 3 pairs but 95 %
     [-4.45, +0.32], which includes zero: not resolved at n=3.
     Locking the memory clock alone: step() +1.80, 95 % [+0.01, +3.59],
     graphics clock down to ~1.0-1.2 GHz. The driver's "Prefer maximum
     performance" for the program: no measurable effect.

Everything below is from output the operator pasted in the session. The

evidence.sha256 exists for them.

prompt positions' dnproj and one prefill lm_head. The bytes fit it too:

--- 3. the kernel alone -----------------------------------------------------

c/tests/bench_dense_gemv_cuda.cu runs the same kernel (quant_matmul_launch)
on the shapes and matrix counts of one token, distinct matrices so L2 (48
MB) cannot serve them, for each COLI_CUDA_I8_ROWS width, three rounds.
Each shape is n device copies of one random int8 matrix, run one shape
at a time, not in the engine's interleaved order.
Every width matched R=0 byte for byte and R=0 matched a double CPU
reference (rel rms 1.0e-07 to 1.1e-07) in all three builds. "batch" is
back-to-back launches between two events; "single kernel" is one launch
between two events, synchronized per call; "single host" is the host wall
around that event, launch and synchronize; "call" is the engine's
coli_cuda_matmul with both pageable copies. Per token, 81 calls, median
of three rounds, ms (first run, 46402074):

  R | batch | single kernel | single host | call
  0 |  7.69 |          8.38 |        9.11 | 9.74
  2 |  3.15 |          3.86 |        4.63 | 5.20
  4 |  3.21 |          3.91 |        4.63 | 5.27
  8 |  3.26 |          3.95 |        4.68 | 5.28

At R=2, all shapes, the first run reads 482-621 GB/s in batch and 323-603
in single kernel (lm_head 603-621 and 595-603). The two later builds
reran this table: R=2 call 5.43 and 5.46.

So a call that costs ~64 us alone (5.20 / 81) costs ~133 in the profiled
engine. Of the ~69 us difference, ~34 is kernel (82 against 47.7 =
3.86 / 81 in "single") and ~35 is the copies and host residue. The
engine's 274 GB/s kernel reading was close enough to the benchmark's R=0
"single" (181-235 GB/s) to raise the question of the DLL's width; the
Nsight grids settle it as R=2 (section on the DLL above).

--- 4. hypotheses for the gap ------------------------------------------------

4a. The spinning OpenMP team. qwen36-attnproj-place-2026-09-22-raw.txt
    left open a "(c) launch latency and OpenMP" for another delta and
    proposed "Rerunning arm A alone with KMP_BLOCKTIME=0" (its line 189);
    this addresses the OpenMP half of that in a different setup.
    Profiled runs, alternated A B A B, A = default (libomp spins 200 ms),
    B = KMP_BLOCKTIME=0:

      run  step()  dense kernel  dense wall  kernel GB/s
      A1   33.1    903 ms        1473 ms     255
      B1   49.8    1072          1698        214
      A2   32.5    887           1449        259
      B2   50.4    1131          1764        203

    Stopping the spin does not speed the dense path; it slows it, and the
    token by ~17 ms. That does not support the spinning team as the
    cause, but it cannot reject it on its own: the B runs decode at ~50
    ms/token, so they spend longer at low utilization and, by section 5,
    longer in P3, which slows the kernel by itself. Two runs per arm,
    unpaired. The stronger argument against host issue delay is section
    5's Nsight table, measured on the GPU timeline.

4b. Short idle gaps between calls. The benchmark (bae8b0c8) spins the host
    0, 50, 200 or 500 us before each call, the gap not counted. Per token
    at R=2, call column: 5.48, 5.57, 5.79, 6.86 ms. A third build
    (30c63e86) read 6.15, 6.03, 6.12, 7.45 in a noisier session: in both
    builds single rounds of dnproj, dnout, attnproj and attnout ran up to
    twice as slow as their neighbours, possibly short P3 episodes (clocks
    were not logged during those runs). Gaps of at most 500 us inside
    stretches of under a second, between saturating sections, cost little.
    That is not the regime of section 5, where the card is under-used for
    seconds on end; no benchmark arm reproduced that.

4c. A fresh output buffer. step() mallocs the vocab-sized logits (~1 MB)
    per token and the caller frees them. The benchmark (30c63e86) timed
    lm_head's call into a reused buffer, into a buffer malloc'd per call,
    and into the reused one plus a memcpy: 998-1002, 1000-1009 and
    1021-1024 us over three rounds. No effect.

4d. Memory clock alone: see section 6.

--- 5. what the card does during decode -------------------------------------

nvidia-smi --query-gpu=timestamp,pstate,clocks.gr,clocks.mem,
utilization.gpu every 500 ms, during the four runs of 4a, then during one
whole benchmark run (the 46402074 build, 38 samples, ~19 s). States by
count of samples (engine runs include model load and exit):

  engine     P2 10251 MHz 62 | P3 5001 MHz 55 | P8 405 MHz 48 |
             P5 810 MHz 44 | P2 810 MHz 1
  benchmark  P2 10251 MHz 37 | P8 405 MHz 1

The sequence in time, one run (A1, log last written 15:28:49), memory
clock, consecutive samples collapsed:

  15:28:45  P2   810 MHz   x 1   gpu 73 %
  15:28:46  P2 10251 MHz   x 3   gpu 25 %
  15:28:47  P3  5001 MHz   x11   gpu 17 %
  15:28:53  P5   810 MHz   x 5

The P3 stretch runs past the end of the run, into the model load of the
next. A1's decode is 127 x 33.1 ms = 4.2 s, so if the log's last write
marks its end it started near 15:28:45: about 1.5 s at 10251 MHz, then
about 2 s at 5001 MHz. Decode start is not marked in the logs, so the ~1.5
s is measured from the P2 burst that precedes it, not from its first
token; it could be a hysteresis timer running from the warm start (the
benchmark header, bae8b0c8, says "~1.5 s"). Every run with a clock log
shows the same shape: the other three of 4a, the N runs of 6a and 6b,
both arms of 6c. Section 1, section 2 and the Nsight run had no clock
log. The shape: a
P2 burst of 1.5-2 s, then P3 (memory 5001 MHz, graphics ~1700-2050 MHz,
utilization 15-40 %) until after exit, then P5 and P8. "About half of a
128-token decode in P3" is this arithmetic, not a per-run measurement.

Nsight Systems 2026.3.2 (nsys profile -t cuda --sample=none
--cpuctxsw=none, one unprofiled run, then nsys stats cuda_gpu_trace
grouped by kernel and grid) gives the kernel durations on the GPU
timeline, where host issue delay cannot enter:

  kernel (grid)                calls  median   min    benchmark, alone
  lm_head       (124160)         128  1647 us  815    ~850 (batch/single)
  dnproj        (6144)          4560    84     42     ~44-55
  attnproj      (4608)          1270    64     32     ~34-45
  dnout+attnout (1024)          5080    30     15     ~17-27

In every row the median is about twice the minimum, and the minimum is
at or just below the benchmark's time. A median at twice the minimum
means more than half of these calls ran slow, more than the "about half
of decode" above, which prefill and profiling differences leave
unreconciled. A minimum over thousands of calls is an extreme,
not the typical P2 call: the durations were not split by time, so the
bimodal shape section 5 predicts is not shown, and the clocks were not
logged during this run. dnproj also mixes 750 prompt calls (25 in a row
on the same 25 MB matrix, which a 48 MB L2 may partly serve) with the
3810 of decode; the other rows are decode only (1270 = 127 x 10, 5080 =
127 x 40) but for one prefill lm_head. Section 6a shows the slowdown is
not the memory clock alone: with memory held at 10251 MHz and graphics
at ~1.1 GHz, lm_head stays at 1.62-1.69 ms, so the graphics clock
matters; whether the memory clock matters on its own was not isolated (no
graphics-only lock). P3 lowers both. The top rows of the same table (20553 H2D
copies of 0.524 MB and 0.066 MB, 20553 offset_to_signed_s4 launches) are
the warm start uploading 6851 experts x 3 matrices, before decode (6851
in that run's rows; the 6859 resident of section 1 is another run's
count, and the difference was not worked out).

--- what this means ----------------------------------------------------------

On this machine, in every decode that was logged, the card holds P2 for
a burst of 1.5-2 s and then drops to P3, at 15-40 % utilization; when
that burst starts relative to the first decoded token is not marked. In
P3 the placed dense kernels take about twice their P2 time. Locking both clocks brings lm_head back
to its benchmark time. That accounts for much of the kernel half of the
benchmark-engine gap of section 3, how much is not measured (no locked
run was profiled); the copy and host half (~35 us a call, measured
profiled) is not addressed by it. The double lock recovered 2.07
ms/token, if that number holds, against ~5.6 missing (81 x 133 us
against 5.20 ms). The expert kernels probably slow down too; that was
not read. Earlier records of this path in docs/experiments logged no
clocks, so how much of their decode ran in P3 is unknown.

It is a weak candidate for the factor 2.1 between the kernel readings of
20 and 21 September (docs/qwen36-cuda-tier.md, the note under the dense
round-trip table). Both readings are of the same 5224 calls over 64
tokens, a decode of ~2 s, which by this record would be mostly P2; and
2.1 is more than the 2 that P3 gives here. It would need one of those
runs to start already in P3, for instance right after another run. No
record of those days logged clocks.

Precedents in the repository for the same mechanism on other backends:
docs/vulkan.md:104-106 ("decode dispatches are microsecond bursts that
never ramp DPM on their own; the memory clock can sit parked through an
entire run", with power_dpm_force_performance_level=high as the pin), and
COLI_METAL_SPIN in c/backend_metal.mm:1020-1022, a keep-alive that keeps
trivial GPU work in flight, described there as an experiment to quantify
clock ramp-down, not as a measured fix. Outside it, llama.cpp issue #17880
(github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/17880, read through a web summary
in this session, not checked line by line) reports a GeForce on Windows
11 stuck in P3 during inference, 25 against 37 tokens/s, with the
Control Panel settings not helping.

--- what this run did NOT measure --------------------------------------------

  - Longer generations. At 128 tokens about half the decode was at full
    clocks in the N arms; a long answer would spend a larger share in P3.
    That any gain of 6b grows with length is an extrapolation.
  - step() under the double lock with enough pairs, in a balanced order.
  - A graphics-clock-only lock.
  - Throttle reasons (clocks_event_reasons), power and temperature: idle
    DVFS against power or thermal capping, in particular for 6a's graphics
    clock at ~1.1 GHz under a memory lock.
  - Clocks during the Nsight run, and kernel durations split by phase.
  - The per-call breakdown of section 2 without the profiler, and whether
    P3 also slows the small h2d/d2h copies.
  - The power and thermal cost of running locked.
  - Any replication in another session or after a reboot.
  - The heat table. Every run here is without one.
  - The generated text in sections 2 to 6 (no guard script compared it).
  - The expert kernels' own slowdown in P3: the graph path records no
    timing without COLI_CUDA_PROFILE, and Nsight's rows for it were not
    read.
  - The driver version, and whether "CUDA - Force P2 State" (NVIDIA
    Profile Inspector) or the global Control Panel setting behave
    differently from the per-program one.
  - A way to keep the clocks up without administrator rights.
